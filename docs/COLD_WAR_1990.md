# 1990 naval setting: source ledger

Research checked 24 September 2026. These are public historical identity and equipment sources for fictional game content. Fictional NATO–USSR combat must be labeled **alternate history, 1990**. These sources do not establish that the invented deployments or battles happened. Numeric detection ranges, hit chances, damage, and readiness are gameplay abstractions, not validated historical performance.

## Rules that prevent anachronisms

- No Arleigh Burke in 1990. The lead ship commissioned **4 July 1991**. [NHHC commissioning record](https://www.history.navy.mil/content/history/nhhc/research/archives/Collections/command-operations-reports/ships/a/arleigh-burke-ddg-51-i.html).
- No ESSM. Navy budget documentation says initial operational capability was **spring 2004**. Use the period RIM-7 Sea Sparrow fit where applicable. [FY2014 Navy weapons budget, ESSM item](https://www.secnav.navy.mil/fmc/fmb/Documents/14pres/WPN_BOOK.pdf).
- No SM-6. Initial operational capability was **November 2013**. [NAVSEA, 29 February 2016](https://www.navsea.navy.mil/Media/News/Article/685151/navy-successfully-tests-new-sm-6-capability/).
- No E-2D. First fleet deliveries began **July 2010**. Use E-2C. [National Naval Aviation Museum](https://www.history.navy.mil/content/history/museums/nnam/explore/collections/aircraft/e/e-2c-hawkeye0.html).
- Avoid MK 54 torpedoes in 1990. Navy's VLA fact file dates MK 54 Mod 0 IOC to **2010**; use period MK 46 for lightweight torpedoes. [Navy VLA fact file](https://www.navy.mil/Resources/Fact-Files/Display-FactFiles/Article/2169310/vertical-launch-anti-submarine-rocket-asroc-vla-missile/).
- Treat each hull's refit as a separate variant. A class name alone does not date its equipment. Commissioning is necessary but does not prove operational readiness on that date.

## US platform ledger

**Ticonderoga cruiser:** CG-47 through CG-51 used Mk 26 launchers. **USS Bunker Hill (CG-52)** commissioned **20 September 1986**, the first Ticonderoga with Mk 41 VLS. Use a clearly labeled VLS variant for CG-52 rather than applying it to CG-47. [NHHC Bunker Hill 1987 command history](https://www.history.navy.mil/content/dam/nhhc/research/archives/command-operation-reports/ship-command-operation-reports/b/bunker-hill-cg-52-ii/pdf/1987.pdf), [NHHC commissioning account](https://www.history.navy.mil/news-and-events/news/2022/nhm-092022.html).

The CG-52 historical entry supports SM-2MR, Tomahawk, eight Harpoon launch canisters, two 5-inch guns, Phalanx, Mk 32 torpedo tubes, and SH-60B. It explicitly records an SH-60B operating aboard immediately after commissioning. Its undated armament header also includes VL-ASROC, so the header must not be copied wholesale as a 1990 loadout. [NHHC Bunker Hill history](https://www.history.navy.mil/research/histories/ship-histories/danfs/b/bunker-hill--cg-52--ii-1986-.html).

**USS Spruance (DD-963), VLS refit:** the 1986–87 overhaul replaced its ASROC box with a 61-cell Mk 41 VLS, upgraded Sea Sparrow for RIM-7M, and added SQR-19 towed array, SH-60B/RAST compatibility, and SQS-53B. A 1990 game using the named DD-963 should use this configuration. Original-fit Spruances can represent earlier or unconverted hulls but need their own identity. The role remains ASW escort, with point air defense, rather than Aegis area defense. [NHHC Spruance history](https://www.history.navy.mil/research/histories/ship-histories/danfs/s/spruance.html).

**Oliver Hazard Perry frigate:** period armament is Mk 13 launching Harpoon and SM-1MR, 76mm Mk 75, Mk 32 torpedo tubes, and Phalanx where fitted. Long-hull variants support SH-60B. The Underwood entry explicitly identifies SM-1MR; Ford and Nicholas entries identify Mk 13 and LAMPS III Seahawk carriage. [NHHC Underwood](https://www.history.navy.mil/research/histories/ship-histories/danfs/u/underwood-ffg-36.html), [NHHC Ford](https://www.history.navy.mil/research/histories/ship-histories/danfs/f/ford-ffg-54.html), [NHHC Nicholas](https://www.history.navy.mil/research/histories/ship-histories/danfs/n/nicholas-iii.html).

**Los Angeles SSN:** first boat commissioned **13 November 1976**. Mk 48 torpedoes and four torpedo tubes are directly supported. Do not grant every Los Angeles hull the later twelve-tube VLS configuration. [NHHC Los Angeles history](https://www.history.navy.mil/research/histories/ship-histories/danfs/l/los-angeles--ssn-688--iv.html).

**Air wing:** the 1990–91 carrier deployment roster supports F-14A, E-2C, A-6E, EA-6B, S-3A/B, F/A-18A/C, SH-3H, and SH-60F, with composition varying by carrier. SH-60B is the surface-escort helicopter; avoid conflating it with SH-60F. [NHHC Gulf War carrier deployments](https://www.history.navy.mil/research/histories/naval-aviation-history/involvement-by-conflict/gulf-war-carrier-deployments.html). First production SH-60B delivery was 28 September 1983. [NHHC 1980–89 aviation chronology](https://www.history.navy.mil/browse-by-topic/communities/naval-aviation/1980-1989.html).

## Soviet platform ledger

[US Navy, Understanding Soviet Naval Developments, July 1991](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GOVPUB-D201-PURL-LPS106117/pdf/GOVPUB-D201-PURL-LPS106117.pdf), especially pp. 55–63 and 129–154, is a near-contemporary reference with an April 1991 information cutoff. Its figures are Western public estimates. It supports:

- Kirov: twenty SS-N-19, SA-N-6, three helicopters; first hull also SS-N-14. Frunze entered service in 1984 and Kalinin in 1988; Yuriy Andropov was still fitting out. Use Soviet-period ship names.
- Slava: sixteen SS-N-12, SA-N-6, SA-N-4, twin 130mm, one helicopter.
- Sovremennyy: eight SS-N-22, SA-N-7, twin 130mm mounts, one helicopter. Surface-warfare emphasis.
- Udaloy: eight SS-N-14, SA-N-9 on later ships, two 100mm mounts, two Helix helicopters. ASW emphasis; avoid an SS-N-22 retrofit. Early ships did not all have operational SA-N-9.
- Victor III, Akula, Sierra and Kilo are period-appropriate submarine classes. The Kilo is a conventional diesel-electric boat, not nuclear.

This source's 1991 fleet totals must not be treated as exact 1990 totals. For a fictional 1990 mission, class-based Soviet identities are safer than unverified hull assignments or modern Russian names.

## Scenario concepts

The Navy's Cold War history describes protection of Atlantic reinforcement shipping and the 1980s Maritime Strategy's greater emphasis on northern European operations. These support fictional convoy escort, Norwegian Sea carrier coverage, and contested-contact ASW missions. [NHHC Cold War history](https://www.history.navy.mil/research/library/online-reading-room/title-list-alphabetically/n/the-navy-in-the-cold-war-era-1945-1991.html).

For game design, give each scenario a task other than destroying every enemy: get transports to a rendezvous, protect a support ship, classify and shadow a contact without firing on neutral traffic, or maintain an ASW screen while reaching a withdrawal point. These are design recommendations, not historical claims.

## Selected hull follow-up

**USS Elrod (FFG-55)** is confirmed as a 1985 Perry frigate with Mk 13, Harpoon/Standard, Mk 46, 76mm, Phalanx, and SH-60B. NHHC's DANFS page and archive landing page conflict over the precise commissioning date, so retain the year rather than a day/month claim. [Elrod DANFS](https://www.history.navy.mil/research/histories/ship-histories/danfs/e/elrod.html), [Elrod archive](https://www.history.navy.mil/research/archives/Collections/command-operations-reports/ships/e/elrod-ffg-55-i.html).

**Otlichnyy** is a suitable Soviet-period destroyer identity: NHHC records it operating near Cuba on 18 January 1986. [John L. Hall history](https://www.history.navy.mil/research/histories/ship-histories/danfs/j/JohnLHall.html). The lead **Sovremennyy** is also documented in trials during 1980. [NHHC photograph USN 1179191](https://www.history.navy.mil/our-collections/photography/numerical-list-of-images/nhhc-series/nh-series/USN-1179000/USN-1179191.html).

**USS Dwight D. Eisenhower** deployed **F-14A+**, rather than the original F-14A, in 1990: VF-142 and VF-143 upgraded in 1989 and made their first deployment with the improved variant in 1990. The A+ was later renamed F-14B. [Naval Aviation News, May–June 1997, p. 11](https://www.history.navy.mil/content/dam/nhhc/research/histories/naval-aviation/Naval%20Aviation%20News/1990/1997/may-june/ascoop.pdf).

**A-6E TRAM with Harpoon** is appropriate. VA-34's history records combat Harpoon employment in March 1986, and a squadron assignment table explicitly identifies the TRAM variant as Harpoon-capable. [NHHC VA-34 history](https://www.history.navy.mil/content/dam/nhhc/research/histories/naval-aviation/dictionary-of-american-naval-aviation-squadrons-volume-1/pdfs/va-34-38.pdf), [NHHC squadron history, p. 211](https://www.history.navy.mil/content/dam/nhhc/research/histories/naval-aviation/dictionary-of-american-naval-aviation-squadrons-volume-1/pdfs/va125-153.pdf). The game's `cw90_a6e` carries the Norden AN/APQ-156 that TRAM was paired with from 1979 and two air-launched AGM-84; its sources and estimates are listed in [DATA_SOURCES.md](../DATA_SOURCES.md#1990-strike-element-2-october-2026).

Specific Victor III hull numbers and the chosen carrier squadron's exact E-2C radar subtype were not established from primary evidence in this pass. Class identity and generic E-2C AEW radar labels are appropriate; unnecessary serial-number precision increases the risk of false authenticity.

## Source pitfalls and bounded gaps

Some NHHC DANFS armament headers contain apparent class-template errors: the CG-47 page lists Mk 41 despite the early ships' Mk 26 fit, Oliver Hazard Perry's header lists ASROC, and Klakring's header lists SM-2. Prefer dated ship-specific command records and the corroborating entries above.

VL-ASROC's commonly reported 1993 service entry was not independently established from a primary source during this bounded pass. Conservative implementation: do not include it in new 1990 loadouts. RUR-5 ASROC is a different, older system.

The Royal Navy's June 1990 Navy News calls Norfolk the first Type 23; September 1991 reports its first live-warhead vertical Sea Wolf firing. Avoid presenting a generic mature Type 23 force in 1990. Type 22/Invincible exact 1990 loadouts were not audited in this pass. [June 1990 Navy News](https://cd.royalnavy.mod.uk/-/media/rnweb/navynews/archivepdfs/1990s/1990/navy-news-june-1990-issue-431.pdf), [September 1991 Navy News](https://cd.royalnavy.mod.uk/-/media/rnweb/navynews/archivepdfs/1990s/1991/navy-news-september-1991-issue-446.pdf).


## Implemented 1990 pack

The three missions form a progression, not a reconstruction of real combat. (A fourth, Baltic mission was cut in M35.) The chronology is a fictional September 1990 NATO–USSR crisis, before German reunification and the Soviet collapse. USS Elrod, Nicholas, Spruance, Dallas, Bunker Hill and Dwight D. Eisenhower use real period identities. Slava and Otlichnyy retain Soviet names. The unknown Victor III and Nanuchka III contacts use class labels because their individual hulls were not established from primary evidence. Civilian ship names are invented. The placement of ships and aircraft, including movement between their historical fleets, is alternate history.

| Mission | Command problem | Victory | Defeat |
|---|---|---|---|
| Northern Convoy | Two Perry escorts must protect one reinforcement merchant while a missile corvette approaches | Cargo reaches its handover box (which may move) and holds there five minutes | Cargo/neutral lost, both escorts lost, or 3-hour deadline |
| The Iceland-Faroe Barrier | Dallas and Spruance localize a transiting Victor III, with a P-3C at Keflavik | Boat (or both boats) neutralized, or 3-hour watch held | A boat reaches the southern exit, or either barrier ship lost |
| Norwegian Sea: Carrier Watch | Eisenhower's detachment and Bunker Hill counter separate Kh-22 raid elements and Slava; four Intruders can strike her | 2-hour watch held and one aircraft recovered; a Harpoon hit on Slava is a bonus | Eisenhower or Bunker Hill lost |

The UI's 20–30 minute durations are initial design estimates for active play with time compression, not measured completion times. The mission deadlines are simulated hours. Northern Convoy starts its cargo ship on a course that intersects the goal, so the player's opening task is protection. The other first-order prompts identify the intended command problem. All three scenarios have fixed seeds, and objectives are tested for victory and defeat through the real loader and mission manager.

### Reactive events (M36)

Each operation's events are written by `tools/scenarios/operation_design.py` and run by the operation director. They fire on the battle rather than a clock, and part of their shape is drawn once per engagement from the scenario seed (a different seed from the menu or `--seed=` draws a different operation). Contact reports are datums with a stated error, never firing solutions; the radio gives the reported position, not the true one. Enemy decisions are not announced. M35's five-minute opening station hold was removed: no movement could fail it, and losing the station ship inside it locked every task.

- **Northern Convoy.** The corvette waits across the route, by the handover box or on the eastern flank. Norwegian coastal radar reports it within a drawn window unless the escort already holds it. When North Star passes the route's midpoint a second contact closes from the north-east: a second Nanuchka or a neutral coaster, reported the same way, so only classification tells them apart. On half the seeds the handover box moves about 8 nm north-west when North Star passes an early point.
- **The Iceland-Faroe Barrier.** The Victor III takes a direct, western or eastern lane. A fixed-array cue arrives within a drawn window unless the barrier already holds the boat. If RED's picture holds Spruance, the boat may stop running and fight (a drawn chance). On some seeds a second boat enters later; it becomes a second breakout loss and joins the neutralization task.
- **Carrier Watch.** Slava's patrol station and the first raid's axis are drawn. The second raid element (one Backfire from the north, two from the north, or one from the east) is sent in a drawn window once RED's own plot has classified the carrier or Slava is hit, otherwise at a drawn latest time. A Norwegian Orion reports Slava within a drawn window; the reserve Intruders are then readied in ten minutes and a Harpoon hit on her becomes a bonus task.

### Enemy mission plans (M36)

The same pass gives the Soviet force a plan (`ai_plans`; the schema is at the top of `scripts/systems/ai_plan.gd`). A plan says what the force is after; it acts only on the Soviet side's own picture, never names a NATO unit and never knows where one is.

- **Northern Convoy.** The corvette is after the merchant: once its own radar has classified one on the route it closes on her and fires at her first, though an escort inside its missiles' reach is still fought. The second corvette, when one comes, joins the hunt.
- **The Iceland-Faroe Barrier.** The Victor III keeps its own breakout. A second boat, when one comes, screens it 4 nm out and fires only at what closes within 8 nm of it.
- **Carrier Watch.** Slava and the Backfires are after the carrier, in the area the raids are sent to: they hold their rounds until their own sensors have classified her, then fire together from bearings either side of the raid axis, within an eight-round budget and a seven-minute assessment; an escort is fired at only inside 20 nm. Later raid elements join on arrival.

## The 1990 strike element

Carrier Watch's detachment is 28 aircraft: 12 F-14A+ (four ready, eight in reserve), 4 E-2C (one ready), 4 A-6E TRAM (two ready, two in reserve until the Orion's report readies them), 4 S-3A and 4 SH-3H. A custom fleet's 1990 Nimitz keeps its nine-aircraft default. Neither is a complete historical air wing: Hornet, Prowler and tanker squadrons are omitted.

Each Intruder carries two AGM-84 with the ship round's 65 nm reach, so a strike can be released outside Slava's SA-N-6. The S-300F's published 25 m engagement floor leaves a sea-skimming Harpoon to her Osa-M and AK-630; even so, an alert, radiating Slava stops most rounds. Measured in the game over eight seeds, all four Intruders (eight rounds) scored a hit in four strikes and two Intruders in none. The choice is therefore a commitment: the strike needs the reserve pair, a located and classified target (the Hawkeye that also watches the raid axes, or a Viking sent to the report), catapult and recovery slots the Tomcats share, and a route outside the SA-N-6; a hit earns the bonus and the attrition but brings the next raid element at once. `tools/opening_plans.py` flies an all-CAP opening and a CAP-plus-strike opening headless against the AI. Before the enemy plan, over ten seeds both won every time (82–85% effectiveness). With the plan (seeds 2 and 13), Slava and the raids fire at the carrier rather than the nearest escort: the all-CAP opening still won, at 77–78%, with Eisenhower taking two or three Bazalt or Kh-22 hits, and the CAP-plus-strike opening won at 92% and 100%, the Intruders hitting or sinking Slava before her later volleys.

## Model boundaries

The catalogue has 21 separate `cw90_` platform variants, 29 weapons and 27 sensors. Modern resources remain available in other missions. `data/cold_war_1990_manifest.json` is the explicit period inventory; the generator and Godot tests recursively validate sensors, weapons, default detachments and scenario loadout overrides. The source ledger establishes broad identities and associations, not every modification on every day in 1990.

- Spruance represents DD-963 after its documented 1986–87 refit. Its 61 VLS cells are recognized, while land-attack Tomahawk stores are outside these maritime missions and omitted from the player-accessible tactical magazine. This does not claim the real ship sailed with empty cells. Neither box ASROC nor later VL-ASROC is inserted into that fit. Bunker Hill likewise lists only mission-available SM-2 and conventional surface/ASW weapons; its unused VLS capacity is not an operational load claim.
- The Perry's 32 SM-1MR and eight Harpoon share the physical 40-round Mk 13 magazine. The engine shares Mk 13 service across SM-1 and Harpoon, including queued and automatic shots. Its eight-second minimum service interval is gameplay tuning, not a verified mechanical cycle. Naval semi-active guidance uses fire-control channels, the shooter's emitting radar and horizon/terrain geometry against held track height. Guidance is lost if that support fails. Illuminator sectors, waveform details and real seeker performance remain abstracted.
- The F-14A+ carries four AIM-54A and two AIM-9M. Its Sparrow stations are intentionally empty because the engine lacks continuous semi-active aircraft illumination. There is no AIM-120 or active-homing AIM-7 substitution. Phoenix terminal acquisition remains a simplified game seeker. The A+ designation is appropriate for the 1990 Eisenhower deployment; it was renamed F-14B later.
- The E-2C uses a generic period AN/APS-series radar baseline because exact squadron radar subtype was not verified. It cannot refuel in the game. SQS-53B hull active sonar and the passive-only SQR-19 towed array are separate sensor bodies so the hull transmitter never borrows the towed body's below-layer position. The S-3A, P-3C and SH-60B deploy sonobuoys; the SH-60B has no dipping sonar. SH-3H and Ka-27PL can dip. Buoy fields abstract processing and communications rather than modelling real acoustic stations. Carrier Watch's 28 aircraft and a custom fleet's nine are scenario detachments, not the full air wing.
- The A-6E TRAM carries the AN/APQ-156 and two AGM-84 Harpoons. Its laser designator, bombs, HARM, SLAM and KA-6D tanking are not represented; speed, endurance and the two-round load are gameplay estimates.
- The Slava fit uses P-500 Bazalt, not P-1000 Vulkan. Its S-300F Fort engages nothing below 25 m, as published. Sovremennyy has eight Moskit and two single-arm Uragan launchers; Udaloy I retains its ASW specialization. The Udaloy catalogue entry represents a later 1980s fit with naval Kinzhal and is not applied indiscriminately to every early hull. Soviet helicopter and submarine magazines are simplified period categories, not an audited ship-specific ordnance return.
- There are no nuclear weapons in these missions. Conventional Kh-22 is distinct from the later Kh-32. Rocket-delivered Rastrub is compressed into an existing torpedo mechanic; its separate anti-surface mode is omitted. Wake and wire guidance, over-the-horizon targeting support, electronic countermeasures, fire-control illumination and cooperative tracking remain simplified. A submerged boat's datalink is disabled, while surfaced/airborne tactical data sharing still uses the game's common faction picture and is more immediate than 1990 operations.
- Range, damage, probability, acoustic signature, sea-state modifiers, fuel/deck cycles and ready quantities are gameplay estimates. Geography uses the checked-in Natural Earth dataset and local projection. Original illustrative recognition models emphasize class silhouette; small fittings and deck layouts are approximate. No blueprints or engineering fidelity is asserted. The charts do not support real navigation; acoustic layers are scenario settings rather than historical oceanographic observations.

Regenerate only this pack with `python3 tools/scenarios/build_cold_war.py`; it uses the same pinned `shapely==2.1.2` dependency as the existing geography builder. The generated resources and scenarios are checked in so playing does not require Python or a network connection.


## Behavioral validation

`docs/validation-cold-war-scenarios.json` records ten runs through the real Main scene in Godot 4.7.2 after the final AI corrections, including domain-matched SAM, torpedo and anti-ship ammunition checks. Both-side AI was exercised with seeds 2 and 13 in every new mission; passive-player seed 2 was also checked in the convoy and ASW barrier. Every run resolved within its scenario deadline, with no script/runtime errors and no actors aground at sampled states. Sandbox log-write and macOS certificate warnings are recorded separately.

The passive convoy reached its goal at 7,313.5 simulated seconds with automatic defenses handling the corvette's six missiles. Dallas prosecuted its local sonar contact and won both AI ASW runs at about 1,106 seconds, while the ASW patrol launched three sorties and laid two buoys; a passive ASW player lost at 1,618 seconds. The carrier watch produced one victory and one defeat, with five sorties in each run. The Baltic AI force lost both tested seeds, one to the gate and one to escort loss; its Advanced label reflects a contested missile engagement rather than a guaranteed scripted win. These are reproducible behavior checks, not a claim of measured human completion time or exhaustive balance testing.
